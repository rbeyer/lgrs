Step 1: Enter the coordinate to be converted.

A wide range of formats is supported. 
        Because there is no universal standard for representing point
        coordinates in a string, a wide variety of formats are supported,
        including (but not limited to) the examples below. However, note that
        only decimal degrees are supported for `LatLonPoint`.
            `"45 120"` -> `LatLonPoint(45, 120)`
            `"45.0° 120.0°"` -> `LatLonPoint(45.0, 120.0)`
            `"-45.0° +120.0°"` -> `LatLonPoint(-45.0, 120.0)`
            `"45.0°S, 120.0°W"` -> `LatLonPoint(-45.0, -120.0)`
            `"N 500000 197819"` -> `LpsPoint("N", 500000, 197819)`
            `"N 500000E 197819N"` -> `LpsPoint("N", 500000, 197819)`
            `"N500000E197819N"` -> `LpsPoint("N", 500000, 197819)`
            `"23 N 250000.0 0.0"` -> `LtmPoint(23, "N", 250000.0, 0.0)`
            `"23 N 250000.0 E 0.0 N"` -> `LtmPoint(23, "N", 250000.0, 0.0)`
            `"23N250000.0E0.0N"` -> `LtmPoint(23, "N", 250000.0, 0.0)`

        When, and only when, cardinal directions are included, longitude-first
        is also supported.
            `"120 W 45 S"` -> `LatLonPoint(-45.0, -120.0)`

        Conversely, the only deviation from the LGRS standard that is
        accommodated for box coordinates is the inclusion of space delimiters.
            `"AZS1359008480"` ->
                `LpsLgrsBox("A", "Z", "S", "13590", "08480")`
            `"AZS13590848"` ->
                `LpsLgrsBox("A", "Z", "S", "1359", "0848")`
            `"AZSN59H48"` ->
                `LpsAccBox("A", "Z", "S", "N", "59", "H", "48")`
            `"42SAM2468910101"` ->
                `LtmLgrsBox(42, "S", "A", "M", "24689", "10101")`
            `"42 S A M 24689 10101"` ->
                `LtmLgrsBox(42, "S", "A", "M", "24689", "10101")`
            `"42 SAM 24689 10101"` ->
                `LtmLgrsBox(42, "S", "A", "M", "24689", "10101")`

If your coordinate is for an LGRS or ACC box, its reference (lower-left) corner will be used by default. Optionally, you can choose to have its center point used instead.

Step 2: Enter the precision, in meters.

This is the same as the side length of each LGRS or ACC box. Supported precisions are 25 km, 1 km, 100 m, 10 m, and 1 m. If you specify an unsupported precision >1 m, it will be rounded down to the closest (finer) precision that is supported. A value <1 m produces an error.

Step 3: Enter a JSON option or the target address in the structured tree.

For example, "json", "json_full", or "nominal.acc.string". 
    Both `"json"` and `"json_full"` give JSON representations of the full 
    conversion tree (described below). The difference is that each
    coordinate in `"json"` is represented by a coordinate string only 
    whereas in `"json_full"`, each coordinate is a JSON object with its own
    elements. Alternatively, you can specify a value directly by giving its
    path in the `"json"` or `"json_full"` tree, such as 
    `"nominal.acc"`, `"nominal.acc.condensed"`, or `"forced.lps.string"`.

    If you're curious to see what elements are available for targeting, just
    generate an example with `"json"` or `"json_full"` and use that as your 
    reference. (For example, try the input coordinate `"80 4"` to see a 
    fully populated tree.) Note that when an unpopulated target is 
    specified, `undefined` is returned, but a target that does not exist
    (for example, because of a misspelling) produces an error.

    For most locations, only one family among `lps`, `ltm_1`, and `ltm_2` is
    populated. However, near zone boundaries, two or all three may be 
    populated. When all families are relevant, the overall structure is as 
    shown below. For easy reference, each attribute name representing a 
    family is shown in square brackets (for example, `[nominal]`, even 
    though the attribute name is `nominal`) and comments are shown in 
    angle brackets:

        GeoRelatives
        ├── input_coordinate                   <parsed input>
        ├── precision                          <same as input>
        ├── extended_ltm                       <same as input>
        ├── use_center                         <same as input>
        ├── sort_by_center                     <same as input>
        ├── note                               <same as input>
        ├── latlon                             <derived from input>
        ├── [nominal]
        │   ├── point: LpsPoint | LtmPoint     <same location as `latlon`>
        │   ├── lgrs: LpsLgrsBox | LtmLgrsBox  <contains `latlon`>
        │   ├── acc: LpsAccBox | LtmAccBox     <contains `latlon`>
        │   ├── corner: LpsPoint | LtmPoint    <`lgrs`/`acc` lower-left corner>
        │   └── center: LpsPoint | LtmPoint    <`lgrs`/`acc` center>
        ├── [lps]
        │   ├── point: LpsPoint                <same location as `latlon`>
        │   ├── lgrs: LpsLgrsBox               <contains `latlon`>
        │   ├── acc: LpsAccBox                 <contains `latlon`>
        │   ├── corner: LpsPoint               <`lgrs`/`acc` lower-left corner>
        │   └── center: LpsPoint               <`lgrs`/`acc` center>
        ├── [ltm_1]
        │   ├── point: LtmPoint                <same location as `latlon`>
        │   ├── lgrs: LtmLgrsBox               <contains `latlon`>
        │   ├── acc: LtmAccBox                 <contains `latlon`>
        │   ├── corner: LtmPoint               <`lgrs`/`acc` lower-left corner>
        │   └── center: LtmPoint               <`lgrs`/`acc` center>
        ├── [ltm_2]
        │   ├── point: LtmPoint                <same location as `latlon`>
        │   ├── lgrs: LtmLgrsBox               <contains `latlon`>
        │   ├── acc: LtmAccBox                 <contains `latlon`>
        │   ├── corner: LtmPoint               <`lgrs`/`acc` lower-left corner>
        │   └── center: LtmPoint               <`lgrs`/`acc` center>
        └── [forced]
            ├── lps: LpsPoint                  <same location as `latlon`>
            └── ltm: LtmPoint                  <same location as `latlon`>

Step 4: Modify advanced options. (optional)

"Extended LTM" extends the Lunar Transverse Mercator (LTM) zones to 82° N/S instead of 80° N/S. "Sort by center" matters only near a boundary between two LTM zones, where a box in each zone can contain the coordinate: the second of the two (ltm_2) is then the box whose center (checked) or reference corner (unchecked) is farther from the coordinate. "Custom note" is passed through to the output, as note.
